# Paper Reading Notes

These notes state exactly how each paper may be used. Do not use a paper beyond the listed supported point.

## Heiderscheit et al. 2011

- Use for: step-rate manipulation in running and the expected direction of loading-related variables when step rate is increased at constant speed.
- Supports: metronome/audio cue can impose step-rate changes; step-rate increases can reduce step length, braking impulse, peak vertical GRF at higher step rate, and impact transient occurrence.
- Use in this project: contrast evidence for our metronome shock proxy increase. It does not support saying our shock increased because participants failed to adjust stride length.
- Do not use for: direct support of "metronome increased shock" or "strong push-off compensation."

## Yong et al. 2014

- Use for: linking more plantarflexed ankle posture in running to foot strike strategy and gastrocnemius activity differences.
- Supports: forefoot strike pattern involves a more plantarflexed ankle at contact and different gastrocnemius activity from rearfoot strike.
- Use in this project: mechanistic support that increased PF peak may reflect ankle posture or strike strategy changes.
- Do not use for: metronome-specific effects or proof of push-off force/power increase.

## Hamner et al. 2010

- Use for: general running propulsion biomechanics.
- Supports: plantarflexor muscles, especially soleus/gastrocnemius, are important in running support/propulsion mechanics.
- Use in this project: background mechanism only, after clearly stating we did not measure ankle power, plantarflexor moment, propulsive GRF, or EMG.
- Do not use for: proving PF angle peak equals push-off power.

## Thaut et al. 1993

- Use for: rhythmic auditory cueing can affect temporal stride parameters and gastrocnemius EMG patterns in hemiparetic walking.
- Use in this project: indirect background only for auditory cueing and motor timing.
- Do not use for: healthy running, plantarflexion peak, or push-off increase.

## Chinn et al. 2014

- Use for: ankle taping can alter ankle kinematics during walking/jogging in chronic ankle instability.
- Use in this project: supports taping-related ankle kinematic constraint/position change.
- Do not use for: shock absorption or proprioceptive improvement.

## Raymond et al. 2012

- Use for: caution against claiming tape/brace improves proprioceptive acuity.
- Use in this project: contrast/caution for any explanation that taping improved step regularity through proprioception.
- Do not use for: supporting proprioception-mediated improvement.

## Cordova et al. 2000

- Use for: ankle support/taping ROM restriction.
- Use in this project: supports the interpretation that taping directionally reduced ankle ROM/dorsiflexion.
- Do not use for: direct shock reduction or step CV mechanisms.

## Terrier and Deriaz 2012 / Repp and Su 2013

- Use for: external rhythmic cueing and sensorimotor synchronization as temporal-control background.
- Use in this project: indirect support that rhythmic cueing can affect timing dynamics.
- Do not use for: direct proof that our T->T+M CV increase was caused by synchronization burden.
